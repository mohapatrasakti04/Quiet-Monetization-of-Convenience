# How Zomato & Swiggy Quietly Monetized Convenience
### How a ₹2 Fee Became a ₹2,000+ Cr Revenue Engine in a Duopoly Market

![Cover](assets/00-cover.png)

A strategy case study on how India's two dominant food-delivery platforms converted a near-invisible line item into a high-margin, industry-wide revenue lever — without adding a single new feature for consumers.

**Author:** Sakti Mohapatra | mohapatrasakti04@gmail.com

---

## 1. Executive Summary

![Executive Summary](assets/01-executive-summary.png)

In April 2023, Swiggy silently introduced a ₹2 "platform fee" on every food delivery order. Zomato followed four months later. By March 2026, both platforms charge ₹14.90 per order (₹17.58 inclusive of 18% GST) — a **645% increase in roughly three years**.

This case study examines how that fee evolved from a negligible add-on into a **₹2,000+ Cr/year combined revenue engine**, and what it reveals about pricing power in a two-player market.

**Core thesis:** In a duopoly with ~99% combined market share, neither player needs to compete on price — they need only move in lockstep. The platform fee is less a product decision than a **pricing-discipline mechanism**, and its success rests on a behavioral insight as much as a financial one: individually small increases don't trigger the resistance that one large increase would.

## 2. Market Context — Why This Was Possible

![Market Context](assets/02-market-context.png)

Three structural conditions made this monetization play viable, using a market-structure lens (size, penetration, concentration):

| Condition | Evidence |
|---|---|
| **Large, still-underpenetrated market** | India's online food delivery market is ~₹79,000 Cr (~$9.1B) in FY25, projected to exceed $27B by FY30 at a 19–23% CAGR — yet online penetration of total food services is only ~11%, versus 58–62% in the US and 42–46% in China |
| **Duopoly concentration** | Zomato (55–58%) and Swiggy (42–45%) together command ~95% of the market — combined daily orders exceed 4.5 million |
| **Low switching pressure** | With only two credible players, a consumer dissatisfied with one fee structure has nowhere meaningfully cheaper to go |

**Strategic read:** high growth headroom + near-total concentration is close to a textbook setup for coordinated (if not explicitly collusive) pricing power — neither platform bears first-mover risk because the other reliably follows.

## 3. Strategic Rationale — Why the Fee Was Introduced

![Why Platform Fee](assets/03-why-platform-fee.png)

Five forces converged, each reinforcing the case for a direct, high-margin revenue lever:

- **Funding quick commerce** — Blinkit alone added 368 dark stores in two quarters amid the Blinkit–Instamart–Zepto war; platform fee revenue helped subsidize that expansion
- **Near-100% margin revenue** — unlike delivery fees, the platform fee carries no attached operational or delivery cost; it flows straight to the bottom line
- **Investor pressure** — Zomato listed in July 2021, Swiggy in November 2024; public markets reward predictable, expanding margins over growth-at-all-costs
- **Path to profitability** — Zomato turned profitable in Q1 FY24 (June 2023), the same quarter the platform fee was introduced, suggesting it was a deliberate profitability lever rather than incidental
- **Commission ceiling reached** — restaurants already pay 25–35% commission; a 2021 NRAI complaint to the CCI (with a 2024 CCI probe finding evidence of unfair practices) meant raising commissions further risked regulatory and restaurant backlash. Charging **consumers** directly avoided that exposure entirely

**Strategic read:** this is a load-shifting decision. Rather than extract more from restaurants (a relationship already under regulatory scrutiny), both platforms redirected the additional margin ask to consumers — the stakeholder segment with the least collective bargaining leverage.

## 4. Execution — The Fee Timeline

![Fee Timeline](assets/04-fee-timeline.png)

The rollout itself is a study in duopoly pricing discipline: **one platform moves, the other follows within weeks**, keeping the two in near-lockstep throughout.

| Period | Swiggy | Zomato |
|---|---|---|
| Apr 2023 | ₹2.36 | — |
| Aug 2023 | ₹2.36 | ₹2.36 (entry) |
| Nov 2024 | ₹11.80 | ₹11.80 (66% spike) |
| May 2026 | ₹17.58 | ₹17.58 |

Neither platform undercut the other at any point in three years. In a genuinely competitive market, one player holding fees lower would be a natural share-gaining move — the absence of that move across 36 months is itself evidence of tacit pricing coordination rather than independent competitive response.

## 5. Financial Impact (FY 2025 Snapshot)

![Financial Snapshot](assets/05-financial-snapshot.png)

| Metric | Zomato | Swiggy |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ₹20,243 Cr | ₹15,227 Cr |
| Profit / Loss | ₹527 Cr (profit) | −₹3,117 Cr (loss) |
| Revenue growth | 67% | 35% |
| Platform fee revenue | ₹235 Cr | ₹221 Cr |
| Average order value | ₹440 | ₹470 |
| Market share | 57% | 42% |
| Market cap | ₹250,000 Cr | ₹72,000 Cr |

**Strategic read:** Zomato leads on every axis — revenue, profitability, growth, and valuation — despite near-identical platform fee revenue to Swiggy. This suggests the platform fee is a **margin lever layered on top of an already-stronger core business**, not the source of Zomato's competitive advantage. For Swiggy, still posting a ₹3,117 Cr loss, the fee is closer to a **profitability lifeline**.

## 6. Revenue Guesstimate — The "₹1 = ₹1,000 Cr Rule"

![Revenue Guesstimate](assets/06-revenue-guesstimate.png)

A back-of-envelope sensitivity model, built the way a consultant would size an opportunity before a client conversation:

```
Combined daily orders (approx.)     = 45 lakh+ (Zomato 25L + Swiggy 20L)
Current platform fee                = ₹14.90/order (excl. GST)
Daily revenue (Zomato alone)        = ₹14.90 × 25,00,000 ≈ ₹3.73 Cr/day
Annualized (Zomato alone)           = ₹3.73 Cr × 365 ≈ ₹1,360 Cr/year
Marginal impact of a ₹1 fee hike    = ₹1 × 45,00,000 × 365 ≈ ₹164 Cr/year (combined)
```

At current order volumes, a further hike to ₹20/order could push **combined industry revenue past ~₹3,200 Cr annually** — illustrating why, at this scale, a "trivial" ₹1–2 fee adjustment is anything but trivial to the P&L.

## 7. The Behavioral Mechanism — "Boiling Frog" Pricing

![Boiling Frog Strategy](assets/07-boiling-frog-strategy.png)

The 645% increase did not arrive as one price shock — it arrived as roughly eight incremental steps, each small enough to read as "barely noticeable" or "just ₹1 more" in isolation:

₹2 → ₹3 → ₹5 → ₹10 → ₹14.90

For context, the fee grew **~35x faster than CPI inflation** (~18% cumulative CPI vs. 645% fee growth) over the same window — a gap that would almost certainly have triggered visible consumer pushback if introduced as a single jump.

A worked example makes the compounding effect concrete: on a ₹400 order, platform fee, delivery fee, surge charges, packaging, and restaurant markup together bring the total to **₹547.58 — 37% above the base food value**, before the consumer necessarily registers the cumulative effect of each individual line item.

**Strategic read:** this is textbook behavioral pricing — anchoring each increase against the *previous* price point rather than the *original* one, so every step reads as marginal even though the cumulative shift is anything but.

## 8. Industry Benchmarking — Not Just a Food Delivery Story

![Industry-wide Fees](assets/08-industry-wide-fees.png)

Platform, handling, and convenience fees have since spread across adjacent consumer internet categories in India:

| Platform | Sector | Fee Introduced | Current Fee (2026) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Myntra | Fashion e-commerce | Jun 2020 | ₹25/order |
| Swiggy | Food delivery | Apr 2023 | ₹17.58/order (incl. GST) |
| Zomato | Food delivery | Aug 2023 | ₹14.90 + GST ≈ ₹17.58 |
| AJIO | Fashion e-commerce | ~2024 | ₹19/order |
| Zepto | Quick commerce | Mar 2024 | Removed Nov 2025 |
| Blinkit | Quick commerce | ~2024 | ₹4/order (handling) |
| Instamart | Quick commerce | ~2024 | ₹9.80/order (handling) |
| BigBasket | Grocery delivery | ~2024 | ₹15 (small orders) |

**Strategic read:** Myntra's 2020 rollout predates Swiggy's by three years, suggesting food delivery didn't originate this playbook — it borrowed a proven one from fashion e-commerce. Zepto's reversal is the one counter-example in the set, worth noting as evidence the model isn't universally durable once a category's competitive dynamics shift (Zepto faced more direct, better-funded rivalry in quick commerce than Zomato/Swiggy face in food delivery).

## 9. Strategic Outlook — Open Tensions

![Road Ahead](assets/09-road-ahead.png)

Four unresolved tensions define where this goes next:

1. **Revenue growth vs. consumer trust** — can monetization keep scaling without eroding long-term loyalty?
2. **Profitability vs. ecosystem health** — do rising margins come at the cost of restaurant-partner sustainability?
3. **Convenience vs. consumer resistance** — how long will consumers keep absorbing rising costs in exchange for convenience?
4. **Growth vs. affordability** — can order growth continue without further fee increases doing the heavy lifting?

## 10. Conclusion

The Zomato–Swiggy platform fee is a case study in what pricing power looks like when a market is large, underpenetrated, and controlled by two players who have more to gain from parallel restraint than from price competition. Three factors made the ₹2 → ₹17.58 climb possible and durable: **structural** (near-95% duopoly control removed competitive price pressure), **financial** (near-zero marginal cost meant the fee dropped almost entirely to the bottom line, directly aiding Zomato's and Swiggy's post-IPO profitability narratives), and **behavioral** (incremental, "boiling frog" pricing kept any single increase below the threshold of visible consumer backlash).

The open question — and the one worth watching — is durability. Zepto's reversal of its platform fee shows the model isn't unconditionally sustainable; it holds only as long as the underlying market stays a duopoly and consumers keep valuing convenience over the now-37% markup it carries. If either condition breaks, the "boiling frog" strategy risks becoming visible all at once.
